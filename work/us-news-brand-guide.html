CASE STUDY · BRAND SYSTEMS & DESIGN OPS · U.S. NEWS & WORLD REPORT

First the book. Then the system that reads it.

A brand guidelines book with 54 pages and 12 chapters. Built with senior stakeholders, reviewed by legal, and signed off by the VP, on a project nobody had time for. Then it was turned into the machine readable design system the company now uses to create on brand work with AI. This page tells both halves of that story.

OVERVIEW

The project in 90 seconds

Before this book, the brand lived in scattered decks, old PDFs, and people's memories. The goal was simple. Put everything in one place: voice, logo rules, color, type, photo, video, social, email, and AI policy. Get it approved by legal and leadership. Then make it useful in the age of AI assisted design.

The artifact

  • A brand guidelines book with 54 pages and 12 chapters
  • Covers foundations, logo and badge rules, and a complete visual identity system
  • Includes channel guidelines and a closing chapter on AI and the brand

The teamwork

  • Built cross functionally with senior stakeholders
  • Reviewed by the legal team for licensing and usage language
  • Taken through multiple VP review rounds to sign off

The persistence

  • This was not an official priority
  • It moved forward in the margins, over a long period
  • A brand without a source of truth loses consistency every day

The payoff

  • The book became the foundation of the company's Claude Design system
  • Its tokens, type scale, and rules were translated into a machine readable system
  • That system is used to create on brand design work with AI

FROM BOOK TO SYSTEM

From book to system

A brand guide that only humans can read solves half the problem. The second half: translate it into a design system an AI can execute.

01

Tokens, not paragraphs

The full palette, the two family type scale, and the spacing and elevation rules were restructured from prose into named tokens and constraints a design tool can apply without guessing.

02

Rules that execute

"Warm colors only for CTAs." "White holds 60%." "Clear space equals one third of the logo height." Written as enforceable defaults, these stop being reminders and start being behavior.

03

One source, two readers

The same source of truth now serves people and AI. Designers cite the book. The Claude Design system applies it. Chapter 12, AI and our brand, made that pipeline policy, not improvisation.

What's actually inside the system

Every token and rule below comes straight from the book. The system reads them and puts them to work:

  • Brand foundations: voice and tone, logo usage, and the full color set, split into neutrals, primary and secondary, and semantic colors like success, warning, and error
  • Type and spacing: the Montserrat heading scale and Roboto body styles, plus an 8 point spacing grid and a small set of corner radius rules, so nothing is placed by guesswork
  • Real components: cards, chips, and badges, alerts, dialogs and tooltips, buttons and icon buttons, form controls, and tabs, each one built to match the brand rules exactly
  • Accessibility built in: keyboard focus rings, labeled form fields, and screen reader support are part of every component by default, not an extra step
  • A working example: a full insurance page built only from these components, showing the system holding up on a real, busy layout

Why this matters more than another Figma file

  • A Figma file is a picture of the brand. Someone still has to look at it, understand it, and rebuild it by hand every time
  • This system is a written set of rules an AI tool can read directly, so it can generate a new page, component, or mockup that already follows the brand, without anyone re-explaining the rules first
  • As design tools like Claude Design, ChatGPT, and Gemini become part of everyday work, a brand only stays consistent if its rules are written somewhere those tools can read. This system is that place

IMPACT

What changed

One answer to "what is the hex?"

Color, type, logo, and badge questions now have a citable, versioned answer. No more digging through old decks.

Legal risk, written down

Licensing, distribution, and image rights rules live in legal reviewed language, not folklore.

On brand AI output by default

The Claude Design system built on the book means AI generated design work starts from the brand instead of being corrected toward it.

A pattern that repeats

Draft doc, then stakeholders, then legal, then VP, then ship, then translate. The pipeline is reusable for the next big document.

Impact is described in words on purpose. This page publishes no adoption metrics or usage numbers.

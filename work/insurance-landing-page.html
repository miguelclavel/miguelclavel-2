02 · CONTEXT

The problem: a page that could only be read, never used

FROM THE TICKET

"As a user looking for insurance, I would like to navigate to the specific type of insurance I need so that I can take some kind of action."

Goals: increase form starts into monetized quote forms; increase pageviews to key rankings and guide pages. Acceptance criteria included monetized entry points, a calculators section, trust signals, preservation of all existing links, and delivery across mobile, tablet, and desktop.

Scroll to see the full 9,500px page ↓

The original sectionfront: eight near-identical stacked sections, a survey grid, and "Why Should You Trust Us?" at the very bottom.

No path to action

366 desktop clicks in 15 days and not one could start a quote. The page's only verbs were "read."

Every vertical treated equally

Car, Home, Life, Renters, Pet, Business, Specialty and Travel got identical real estate, while the click data shows one of them owns ~40% of intent.

Trust as an afterthought

The authority story, 90+ years of journalism, unbiased methodology, sat below the 9,000px mark, past where scrollmaps go cold.

04 · RATIONALE

Ten decisions, each with a receipt

Every major choice traces to a heatmap insight, an acceptance criterion, or an explicit trade-off. The six most consequential:

01
Decision 01

Lead with a quote form, not an index

Replace the editorial H1 and intro with a conversion hero: coverage chips, ZIP field, red "Compare →" button.

Evidence

KPI #1 is form starts; the old page had zero form surface; "Cheapest…" links dominated clicks.

Trade-off

Risks reading as an aggregator, countered with an editorial trust row directly under the form.

02
Decision 02

Default to Auto, make ZIP conditional

"Auto" is pre-selected; only Auto, Home, Renters and Pet show a ZIP field, the rest go straight to a quote CTA.

Evidence

~40% of clicks are car-related; ZIP is only collected where the monetized flow needs it.

Trade-off

Slightly biases the UI toward one vertical, accepted, because the data does too.

03
Decision 03

Compress 9,500px into one explorer grid

All eight categories become one card grid with the same 48 sub-links the old page had.

Evidence

938px average fold meant six categories were effectively invisible; acceptance criteria required keeping every link.

Trade-off

Loses the large category photos, imagery moved to guides, where it earns engagement.

04
Decision 04

Make everything clickable actually clickable

Whole-title link targets, hover states on every card, real hrefs on all ~120 links.

Evidence

Dead clicks: 10.4% desktop, 26.6% mobile, users repeatedly clicked non-link headers.

Trade-off

None. This one was free.

05
Decision 05

Move trust next to the ask

Three trust layers: hero trust row, mid-page stats band (1933 · 100+ · $0 · 85K+), and an awards showcase with a transparent "How we scored" weighting panel.

Evidence

Acceptance criterion #5; the old trust block sat below the 9,000px mark, past where scrollmaps go cold.

Trade-off

Spends premium viewport space on non-clickable content, justified as conversion support.

06
Decision 06

Treat SEO as a constraint, not a retrofit

A dedicated audit pass after visual design: restore every production link category, match the title tag, add canonical, Open Graph and six JSON-LD types.

Evidence

The first design draft scored 41/100, a great UX shell with hollow SEO. The ticket demands preservation.

Trade-off

A full extra workstream, and the reason the final scored 88/100 against production's 66.

Also decided

  • Keep the editorial disclaimer but give its "Learn more" link a real destination (it was mobile's #1 dead tap)
  • Label the rate comparison "SAMPLE" rather than fake personal quotes
  • Defer a dedicated calculator module to the roadmap pending product inventory, shown here as an open item, not quietly dropped
05 · THE WORK

The redesign, section by section

Each module answers a specific finding. Screenshots are from the final high-fidelity prototype, built on U.S. News design tokens.

The hero: chips, conditional ZIP, and a trust row in one card.

CONVERT

A quote form as the front door

  • "Compare insurance quotes in real time & save": eight coverage chips plus a ZIP field and a red Compare button
  • Auto is pre-selected
  • Life, Commercial, Motorcycle and Travel skip the ZIP entirely, no field is asked for that the flow doesn't need
  • Validation, loading and success states are fully specified, down to the aria-live announcements

Eight categories, 48 preserved sub-links, one viewport.

ROUTE

Explore insurance by type

  • Eight stacked sections become one grid
  • Every card is a full click target, with an icon and a "Best Companies" ranking link
  • The same five sub-links users already relied on, "Cheapest…" first, because that's what they click

Award winners with their scoring weights exposed.

TRUST · REVENUE

Awards with the math showing

  • The Best Of Awards band doesn't just display badges, it auto-plays through the category winners so visitors see the full lineup without clicking
  • Each slide auto-reveals a "How we scored" breakdown for that winner: Customer satisfaction 40%, Complaint data (NAIC) 25%, Claims resolution speed 20%, Cost & value 10%, Coverage & options 5%
  • Methodology transparency becomes a feature no aggregator can copy
  • These badges are a real revenue line, carriers license the winning seal for their own marketing, so a clear, defensible scoring story protects that business

Guides: three visible, expandable to six, whole-title links.

EDUCATE

Editorial guides and savings tips

  • The pageview KPI survives the redesign
  • Guide cards with real linked titles
  • A "Ways to lower your premium" tips row routes into bundle and discount pages
  • Mid-funnel content that also feeds topical authority

1933 · 100+ · $0 · 85K+, authority in four numbers.

REASSURE

Ratings you can rely on

  • The trust story moves from the page's basement to its midpoint
  • Founded 1933, 100+ carriers rated, $0 cost to consumers, 85K+ data points
  • Placed immediately before the second conversion ask

An honest sample comparison beside the CTA.

MONETIZE

See your rates in about two minutes

  • Shows the shape of the payoff before asking for anything
  • A comparison card explicitly labeled "SAMPLE COMPARISON · AUTO / ZIP 10001"
  • An honest preview reduces the perceived risk of starting the form

Insurance 101: ten terms, passage-rankable.

EXPLAIN

FAQ and a glossary the old page never had

  • Five FAQs with FAQPage schema
  • A ten-term glossary gives first-time buyers a vocabulary
  • Gives AI search engines citable passages
  • Net-new topical authority production lacked
06 · PRESERVATION

Keeping the equity: 66 → 88

The ticket's hardest constraint: "maintaining all of the SEO elements the current page already has." The first design draft failed it. The audit fixed it.

Audit score
Production page
66/100
First design draft
41/100
Final redesign
88/100

Composite audit across UX, internal linking, schema, E-E-A-T, AI-search readiness and Core Web Vitals signals.

What the audit fixed
Resolved · Links

~120 dead href="#" links replaced with real usnews.com URLs, nav, breadcrumb, 8 category cards with 48 sub-links, guides, awards, and the full ~50-link footer taxonomy.

Resolved · Metadata

Missing metadata added: production-matching title tag, meta description, canonical, robots directives, Open Graph and Twitter cards.

Resolved · Schema

Six JSON-LD types added, more than production has: Organization, WebSite + SearchAction, WebPage, BreadcrumbList, FAQPage, ItemList.

Resolved · E-E-A-T

Methodology links made real, award winners entity-linked to their carrier review pages, descriptive anchor text everywhere.

Structured data added
Organization WebSite + SearchAction WebPage BreadcrumbList FAQPage ItemList

Deliberately omitted: Review/AggregateRating schema, it needs real CMS rating data to avoid rich-result penalties. URL slugs follow usnews.com patterns but are flagged for validation against the live sitemap before launch.

08 · ACCOUNTABILITY

What success looks like, honestly

This case study was written at handoff, before launch. There are no invented uplift numbers here, there is a measurement plan, with the heatmap study as its baseline.

Primary KPI

Form starts

  • From structurally zero to a measurable funnel
  • Tracks hero form starts
  • Completion rate and per-coverage-type mix
Secondary KPI

Pageviews to key pages

  • Click-through to "Best Companies" rankings
  • And to guides
  • Measured against the old page's baseline
Experience KPI

Mobile dead-click rate

  • Baseline: 26.6%
  • Every element that looks clickable now is
  • This number should collapse
Guardrail

Bounce rate

  • Desktop baseline: 46.7%
  • If conversion rises while bounce spikes, the form is capturing instead of serving
  • That trade is not acceptable

Open at handoff:

Dedicated calculators module pending product inventory (acceptance criterion #3, tracked, not dropped)

URL slug validation against the live sitemap

Server-side rendering and Core Web Vitals work (font preloads, image dimensions) at the template level

The old page could only be read. The new one can be used, and it can be measured.

Case study · Medical aesthetics training

From Training Catalog to Clear Membership Journey

I helped turn a wide medical aesthetics training offer into a clearer path from course interest to membership. The work connected landing pages, membership choices, classroom materials, email, and recorded content into one consistent experience.

04 · Problem

A strong course catalog without a clear decision path

Visitors could see many valuable courses and still leave without answering the questions that decide an enrollment.

  • Which courses fit my professional goals?
  • Which membership gives me the access I need?
  • What is included at each level?
  • Can I attend in person, online, or through recorded learning?
  • What happens after I enroll?

The operational side

The website, email promotions, classroom slides, banners, and recorded content needed to speak the same language. When each channel was designed independently, the experience could feel fragmented and production took longer.

05 · Audience and trust

Three professionals, three different decisions

Profile 01

Exploring a new service

A licensed professional looking for an introductory course and a low commitment way to begin.

Profile 02

Building confidence

A returning learner comparing several courses, practice support, and continued access.

Profile 03

Expanding a practice

An experienced professional who sees value in broad course access, ongoing education, and premium support.

Trust checklist

Clear eligibility and professional requirements
Accurate course terminology
Transparent inclusions and exclusions
Visible delivery format and schedule
Honest membership comparison
No promises of income or professional outcomes

Eligibility and scope of practice vary by profession and location. Nothing in this program promises employment, income, licensure, business success, or permission to perform procedures.

06 · Funnel diagnosis

Five stages, five design responses

This came from a content and funnel review of the existing pages and campaign material. It was not a formal usability study, and no test or heatmap data was available.

  1. 01

    Discover a course or topic

    Friction: too many individual topics with no grouping.

    Design response

    Grouped course families with plain language names.

  2. 02

    Understand the training format and value

    Friction: format, schedule, and eligibility were hard to compare.

    Design response

    Benefit led summaries with format and date on every card.

  3. 03

    Compare membership options

    Friction: three tiers differed in many small ways at once.

    Design response

    A progressive comparison that starts with fit, then detail.

  4. 04

    Select a plan and enroll

    Friction: trust and terms information sat far from the action.

    Design response

    Eligibility, renewal, and inclusions placed beside enrollment.

  5. 05

    Continue learning through classes and recorded content

    Friction: disconnected materials across channels.

    Design response

    One content system feeding web, email, classroom, and video.

08 · Landing page

One page that answers the qualifying questions in order

Page hierarchy
  1. 01Clear value proposition for licensed professionals
  2. 02Course categories and learning formats
  3. 03Reasons to trust the training
  4. 04Membership comparison
  5. 05Upcoming opportunities
  6. 06Frequently asked questions
  7. 07Enrollment action

UX decisions

  • Repeated calls to action use consistent language
  • Course cards show level, format, location, and next date
  • Membership appears after the user understands course value
  • Trust details sit near the enrollment decision
  • Mobile preserves the decision order and does not collapse critical terms

The close of the page: one form, one action, and the contact details a professional needs before committing.

The full page, read from top to bottom

Chapter 01

Homepage value proposition and workshop discovery

The top of the page states who the training is for, then moves straight to upcoming workshops so a qualified visitor can see something real to attend.

Chapter 02

Course and CME content organization

Topics are grouped into families with the calendar close by, so schedule and subject are compared together rather than on separate pages.

Chapter 03

Virtual learning and recorded content

Formats are explained as options rather than fine print: live, virtual, and recorded learning each get their own explanation on the page.

Chapter 04

Membership and conversion paths

Membership appears after course value is established, with the enrollment action repeated in the same language used earlier on the page.

Chapter 05

Campaign reuse and partner credibility

The lower page carries training locations and partner marks, the same trust material the email campaign reused without redesigning it.

11 · Expert content to clear communication

Clarity came from structure, not from removing context

Before

Course information placeholder. A dense block of technical course information with inconsistent naming and no clear next action. Terminology varies between the schedule, the deck, and the promotional email. Eligibility appears near the end, and the reader has to infer the format, the location, and what happens after enrollment.

After
  • One clear learning goal
  • Essential eligibility information
  • Scannable course details
  • A visible next step
  • Supporting clinical terms preserved through expert review

Clinical instructions were never rewritten in a way that changed their meaning. Structure and hierarchy did the work.

14 · Collaboration with medical professionals

Clinical review as part of the design workflow

Medical content needs both clarity and accuracy. Direct review with subject matter experts helped preserve terminology, confirm claims, and catch issues before the material reached learners.

  1. 01

    Content intake

    Material and objectives collected from the expert.

  2. 02

    Design translation

    Hierarchy, structure, and visual language applied.

  3. 03

    Clinical review

    Terminology and claims checked by the professional.

  4. 04

    Final approval

    Approved version released to every channel.

Remote collaboration note

Clear annotations, file naming, version notes, and approval states reduced uncertainty when several contributors reviewed the same material.

17 · Impact

One clearer journey across web, email, and the classroom

  • Membership differences became easier to understand
  • The premium tier was positioned through relevant value and user fit
  • Course discovery became more structured and scannable
  • Expert material could be reused across more channels with a consistent message
  • Clinical review became a visible part of the design workflow
  • The website and training materials felt like one connected experience

Qualitative outcomes. Numbers will be added only when verified data is supplied.

18 · Reflection

This project taught me that conversion work in professional education starts with trust. People need to understand what they are choosing, why it fits their goals, and what happens next. The strongest solution was not one page or one campaign. It was a shared content system that connected marketing, membership, and learning.

Clarity can support conversion without pressure
Expert review is part of the user experience
Reusable content systems improve both speed and consistency

BLU Products · 2014 to 2019

One phone. One launch system. Every channel.

At BLU Products, I helped turn a fast stream of smartphone releases into clear product stories. Each launch had to work across web, retail, email, social, packaging, events, and partner channels while staying accurate, on brand, and ready on time.

04 · The challenge

Make every phone feel distinct without rebuilding the process every time.

Speed

Several campaigns could overlap, and specifications could change close to launch.

Accuracy

Product images, storage, cameras, colors, connectivity, and retailer copy had to match the final device.

Range

A rugged phone and a fashion focused phone needed different stories even when they used the same production pipeline.

Adaptation

A BLU master campaign still had to fit the rules, dimensions, and customer expectations of large retail partners.

05 · The launch system

A repeatable path from product data to launch ready assets.

  1. 01

    Receive and verify

    Review specifications, early renders, colors, names, and launch requirements with product and overseas teams.

  2. 02

    Find the lead story

    Choose the feature that matters most for this device, such as battery, display, camera, durability, design, or value.

  3. 03

    Build the master campaign

    Define the visual idea, image treatment, hierarchy, product copy, and reusable layout rules.

  4. 04

    Adapt by channel

    Translate the master story into web, email, social, video, packaging, point of purchase, and retailer formats.

  5. 05

    Check production

    Verify sizes, variants, specifications, legal text, retailer rules, exports, and print readiness.

  6. 06

    Release and support

    Deliver organized files, answer production questions, and update assets when the product or channel changes.

07 · Product website

One product page had to explain the phone in seconds and keep rewarding the scroll.

  • Opening

    Strong visual opening with the full device family.

  • Hierarchy

    A clear hierarchy from design to display, performance, camera, color, software, and specifications.

  • Proof

    Feature sections that pair one main claim with one visual proof.

  • Sequencing

    Technical information delayed until the user has context.

  • Rhythm

    A consistent rhythm that supports a very long page.

08 · Web and UX decisions

Product discovery worked best when the page answered one question at a time.

Customer question

What makes this phone different?

Design response

Lead with one memorable feature and a clear product name.

Customer question

Is it right for me?

Design response

Connect specifications to real use without hiding the numbers.

Customer question

What does it look like?

Design response

Show front, back, side, color, and material with consistent render quality.

Customer question

Will it work on my device?

Design response

Keep the hierarchy and CTA usable across desktop, tablet, and phone.

Customer question

Where can I buy it?

Design response

Prepare the campaign for direct and retailer specific paths.

09 · Retail adaptation

One master campaign, many retail environments.

Each partner had its own dimensions, typography rules, copy limits, image requirements, promotional calendar, and customer expectations. Walmart, Best Buy, T Mobile, Sprint, and BrandsMart USA did not treat a product asset the same way. The goal was to preserve the device story while making the asset feel native to the partner's platform.

Brand compliance

Logo use, campaign typography, and product naming stayed consistent with BLU rules.

Product accuracy

Renders, colors, storage, and network details matched the approved final device.

Channel dimensions

Every partner format had its own sizes, safe areas, and copy limits to respect.

Retail readiness

Files were exported, named, and packaged so the partner could publish without rework.

Public context: in September 2018 BLU announced the VIVO XI Plus and VIVO XI as a Best Buy exclusive launch. Retail partnerships were company decisions. My work was the campaign adaptation they required.

12 · Global production

Clear communication protected the work when teams were far apart.

Device renders and technical details could come from production partners in China, while final assembly, branding, sales, and warehouse needs were handled locally. Most costly mistakes were caught by checking the same short list every time, in writing, before anything moved to production.

01Model name and SKU
02Front, back, side, and color accuracy
03Screen content and operating system version
04Camera, memory, display, battery, and network specifications
05Packaging and legal copy
06Retailer naming and file dimensions
07Final export and archive structure

14 · Launch speed

Speed came from a clearer system, not from skipping the details.

01

Start with verified product information.

02

Build a master story before resizing assets.

03

Separate shared campaign rules from retailer specific rules.

04

Package decisions with the files so the next team knows what changed and why.

The work taught me that a design system can be a production system too. Reusable structure made room for better ideas while protecting accuracy under pressure.

15 · Measurement

What success should be measured.

Recommended launch measurement framework. These are definitions I would use on a launch today, not results from this period.

Launch readiness

Approved assets divided by required assets, measured at the release date.

Accuracy

Count of specification or variant corrections raised after final approval.

Retail acceptance

Assets accepted on first submission divided by assets submitted, by partner.

Production efficiency

Revision rounds per asset family and median turnaround time from brief to approval.

Digital engagement

Product page CTA rate, scroll depth, retailer click through, and video completion.

Email performance

Open rate, click through rate, and product page traffic attributed to the send.

Consistency

Channels using the approved master story and final render set, divided by channels in the launch.

17 · Reflection

What I carried forward.

This role taught me to see a launch as one connected experience. The website, package, retail display, email, social post, sales guide, and event booth all shape the same customer understanding. The strongest work came from finding one clear product story, documenting it well, and adapting it with care.
